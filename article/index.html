WORK ARCHETYPES × AI

Your Title Is a Lagging Indicator

The 5 work archetypes of the AI era — based on Boris Cherny's thesis · playable as a 2-minute quiz

Something strange is happening to job titles. The engineer who ships a landing page before standup, the designer who prototypes in code, the PM who runs their own data analysis — the old boundaries between engineering, product, design, and data science are melting. Not because people got more ambitious, but because AI collapsed the cost of crossing them.

Boris Cherny — creator of Claude Code — put words to what comes next. Watching his own team work, he noticed that when titles stop describing what people do, something else starts describing it better:

"As engineering, product, design, DS, etc. melt into a new kind of role, I was reflecting on what roles might look like in the future. When I look at the Claude Code team I see what I think is five archetypes."

The insight is that an archetype isn't a skill set — it's a value-creation pattern. It describes the move you instinctively make when you sit down at work: do you open a blank page, or a backlog? Do you add, or delete? Ship, or tend? Titles are assigned; archetypes are revealed. And in an AI-saturated team, your archetype — not your title — determines where your leverage is.

The five archetypes

1 · Prototyper

explores the blank page

Generates new ideas faster than anyone can evaluate them. Most won't ship — that's the point. The Prototyper maps the option space so the team knows where the gold is.

AI LEVERAGE: Delegate breadth — let agents spin up ten variations while you supply taste and direction. Own idea selection; automate idea production.

2 · Builder

makes it real

Turns ideas into production-grade reality, fast. The Builder defines what "done" means and drives to it — the bridge between demo and dependable.

AI LEVERAGE: Delegate boilerplate and scaffolding; own architecture and the definition of done. Warning: unreviewed agent output makes you the fastest producer of debt.

3 · Sweeper

creates by removing

Simplifies, unships, deletes. The Sweeper creates clarity by subtraction — fewer steps, fewer features, less noise. The rarest archetype, and rising in value.

AI LEVERAGE: Use agents to map complexity and surface candidates to cut; own the judgment of what to remove. AI generates complexity faster than ever — the Sweeper is the antidote.

4 · Grower

iterates toward fit

Takes what exists and iterates it toward what the market actually wants. Signal, experiment, adjust, repeat. The Grower turns launched into loved.

AI LEVERAGE: Delegate experiment setup and data crunching; own the read on what the signal means and which iteration to run next.

5 · Maintainer

keeps it standing

Owns mature systems and relationships and keeps them secure, reliable, and efficient at scale. When nothing breaks — that's the Maintainer, doing the job perfectly.

AI LEVERAGE: Automate the toil — monitoring, routine ops. Own the deep system context and careful review of agent changes for subtle risk. No tool replaces your knowledge of how things actually fail.

You're a blend, not a box

Nobody is one archetype. You're a mix that shifts with context, product stage, and team: pre-product-market-fit work rewards Prototypers; growth-stage products live or die on Growers; every stage needs Builders; and the more AI accelerates output, the more valuable Sweepers and Maintainers become — the people who keep speed from turning into sprawl.

That's also the deeper reading of the thesis: AI doesn't make archetypes obsolete, it sharpens them. Each archetype has a piece that delegates beautifully to agents (production, boilerplate, toil, experiment plumbing) and a piece that absolutely does not (taste, judgment, the definition of done, the read on the signal). Knowing your archetype tells you which side of that line you should be standing on.
